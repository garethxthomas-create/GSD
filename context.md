Edit it whenever the picture changes. It lives in the repo, so any Claude chat can update it too: ask for a new `context.md` and upload it to the main page like any other file.

## What the Sallie Mae projects mean

- **Admin** is the critical path. Background check gates resignation, resignation gates the last day, the last day gates the start. Nothing here is optional and nothing here can slip quietly.
- **Straight answers** is the learning engine. Ten questions an employee would actually ask, answered in Gareth's own words in one accumulating file. See the drill below.
- **Advisor App** is a personal tool being loaded with Sallie Mae context. It needs to do one job well by day one, not ten jobs badly.
- **Week One Prep** is questions, not answers. The artifacts stay in the drawer until they have been tested against reality. Being confidently wrong in week one is expensive; being visibly curious is not.

## The drill (how the learning actually works)

For every Q task in Straight answers:

1. Write the answer first, from what you already know, in plain language, for an employee. No sources open.
2. Where you stall mid-sentence, that is the reading. Go and get exactly that, nothing more.
3. Finish the answer.
4. Add anything you still cannot answer to the "Still cannot answer" list at the bottom of the file.

Done is not "I read the thing". Done is the answer sits in the file and could be said out loud without notes. The gaps become questions for Rick and for week one. The old numbered reading tasks are the source shelf, labeled @reference, and exist to be raided rather than completed.

## The business, in short

Sallie Mae is a private student lender. It lends to families paying for college, and increasingly sells the loans it makes while keeping the servicing and the customer relationship.

Three things shape the communications job:

- **H.R. 1** removed Grad PLUS and capped Parent PLUS from July 1, which expands the private market that Sallie Mae serves. Internally this must never be described as a windfall.
- **Selling loans** sounds to an employee like giving away the company's assets, so it needs explaining in human language rather than the phrase "capital-light".
- **Being a public company** changes what internal comms can say and when. MNPI, quiet periods and Reg FD are new territory after BSI and NNL, which never traded on an exchange.

## What GSD's own words mean

- **Today's Three.** Up to three tasks actually due today. Never padded. If one thing is due, one thing is the honest answer.
- **Week outcome.** The line at the top of every brief, held in Todoist as a task labeled @outcome and dated on that week's Monday. It is the thing the week is for.
- **Strike.** A task that was due and did not get done takes a strike. Three strikes and it goes to the Pit.
- **The Pit of Doom.** Where three-strike tasks go to stop nagging. Nothing there is judged. Friday's review asks whether anything deserves rescue or a clean deletion.
- **@reference.** Source material. Never nagged about, never picked, always available.
- **Later.** Demoting a task with a stated reason. The reason is respected, the task is parked with a visible note, and it comes back when it can actually be done.
- **SHTF.** The day detonated. Say what changed and the afternoon gets rebuilt. The plan changed; you did not fail it.
- **Avoiding.** Circling a task and drifting. Names the resistance, hands over one physical move of ten minutes or less.
- **Focus target.** 90 minutes of deliberate work a day. Not eight hours of feeling busy.
- **Briefing.** The stand-back view, on demand from the dashboard. Where you are against the week's outcome, what moved in the last seven days, what the next week or so holds including any overloaded day, and at most three things that deserve attention now. Ask follow-up questions straight after it.

## How to coach Gareth

Direct, warm, no shame, no corporate language, no em dashes, US spelling. Conclusion first, then only the explanation needed. One clear recommendation beats three weak options. Label inference as inference. Surface conflict rather than smoothing it. Never moralize about what did not get done.

He is a communications professional by trade, so he learns by writing the explanation until it holds up. When he asks about a Q task, help him build his own answer rather than handing him a summary of the source.
